Read off a fresh sync this afternoon, after Week 2 finished and before Week 3 kicks off. You're 4-0 counting the weekly median result, which is the best start in the league alongside sweeneyb and elembach.

Both offers from the weekend were still sitting unanswered. Testing them against fresh data, **the ranking was inverted** — the one marked "optional, only if the other passes" is the better move, and the one marked primary should come off the table.

## The Tally241 offer has a false premise

The weekend report said "Aaron Jones is still on Tally241's bench." He isn't — he's **in their starting lineup**, at flex, and their coach said Monday he's expected to be fine for Sunday after the knee that took him out briefly in Week 2.

That changes the offer completely. Tally241 is 1-3 with Puka Nacua out and Malik Nabers questionable. You were asking a team that badly needs to win now to pull a healthy starting back out of its lineup in exchange for a third quarterback it would never start. That doesn't get accepted, and four days of silence is the expected outcome, not bad luck.

The value side is worse. Kirk Cousins isn't spare parts — he's Las Vegas's starter, he won the job outright in camp, and he's completing 68% with six touchdowns to three interceptions through two games. Aaron Jones would be your fourth back and wouldn't crack the lineup. Trading a productive starting quarterback plus a receiver for a bench body is the wrong direction, whoever says yes.

The weekend report actually got this right in its closing line — "hold the rest of your quarterback surplus; a contender who loses a starter in October will pay more than anyone will today" — and then offered two of the four anyway. That's the contradiction worth fixing.

**Pull it.** And pull it before you send anything else, because a pending offer locks the players inside it: while Cousins is sitting in that offer, you can't put him in a better one.

## The amandaberry offer is the one to send

Same shape, much cheaper, and much likelier to land:

- **They have a real need.** Trevor Lawrence and Jordan Love start every week with no third quarterback behind them.
- **They can spare the player.** Five backs, and Brian Robinson is behind Bijan Robinson on his own team and behind Jahmyr Gibbs on theirs.
- **You spend your least valuable arm.** Bryce Young is the fourth of four quarterbacks and the only one who never starts for you.
- **The pitch is better than the one that was written.** Young's bye is Week 5. Lawrence is off in Week 7 and Love in Week 11. Young covers *both*. The earlier message sold him as generic depth and left the strongest argument on the floor.

What you get is honest depth, not a starter: Robinson has 22 touches for 98 yards in a backup role, and he's dealing with an ankle that had him limited on Monday with Atlanta playing Thursday. Don't expect anything from him this week. He's a body at the one position where your only spare is a receiver sliding into the flex.

## What it costs, and why that cost is smaller than it looked

Trading Young leaves Week 13 without a second quarterback — Lamar Jackson and Cousins are both on bye that week, so a receiver would stand in the superflex spot. That's a real downgrade, and it's the entire reason this offer got demoted to "optional" over the weekend.

It shouldn't have been. Week 13 is eight weeks out, and two unrostered quarterbacks who are listed starters for their teams cover it right now: **Deshaun Watson** (Cleveland, off in Week 11) and **Cam Ward** (Tennessee, off in Week 9). Either one patches that slot for a couple of dollars whenever you want it. A hole you can fill from the wire at will, two months early, is not a reason to prefer a deal that hands away your best trade chip.

Worth noting it's a downgrade, not a failure: Week 13 still fields a legal lineup either way.

## Where Cousins actually has leverage — and why to wait

ObiWanless is the team with a genuine quarterback emergency: Joe Burrow is their only healthy arm, Jayden Daniels is out, and this league starts two quarterback-capable spots. Cousins is worth more to them than to anyone.

The weekend report flagged them and passed because their spare backs are weak. That call was right, and it still is — Devin Singletary and Sione Vaki are worse than what's already on offer, and Kenneth Walker, the back worth wanting there, is a top-six fantasy back on Kansas City taking 82% of the snaps. He isn't coming back for a third quarterback, and asking sets the wrong tone.

So hold Cousins rather than discounting him. The trade deadline is Week 11, eight weeks away — there's no rush, and the market for a starting quarterback gets better every week somebody's starter goes down, not worse.

## One thing to keep an eye on

Your real shortages are a kicker in Week 7 and a defense in Week 10 — one body each, no backup. Neither is a trade problem; both are cheap waiver buys in the week before, and `/waivers` has them scheduled. No trade should be made to solve them.
