> Refreshed Oct 24 after a roster change: Lamar Jackson's status turned Out.

## What changed: Jackson's Out doesn't open a trade, it closes one

A quarterback going Out for a third time in four weeks is exactly the kind of moment a bad trade gets made — either selling him cheap into a down week, or some rival testing whether Ben will panic. Neither is the right read. He still projects as a difference-making starter in Baltimore the moment he's healthy (34.6 points over the rest of the season, 11.1 in the playoffs, more than any other bench player by a wide margin), and this league's trade rule only clears offers Ben is certain are better than holding — selling a banged-up asset into a trough is the definition of the risk that rule exists to screen out. Today's waivers report moves him to IR instead, which costs nothing and keeps the asset. Nothing to send out on him.

## Re-scanned all 12 rosters fresh off today's sync — nothing else moved either

- **Quarterback-for-running-back still doesn't clear the bar.** The same four teams carrying exactly two quarterbacks apiece (GehrmannJake, sweeneyb, ObiWanless, amandaberry) still read as the plausible partners for a spare arm, and the math hasn't changed since Thursday: Cousins for Bhayshul Tuten (sweeneyb) still projects positive rest-of-season but loses value in the playoffs, and every other combination loses value well before the playoffs even enter the picture. Goff and Young aren't spare, and Jackson being temporarily unavailable doesn't change that — if anything it tightens the case for holding all three a little longer rather than thinning out further.
- **Juwan Johnson for a running back still costs more than it fixes.** Same conclusion as the last three hunts: the point edge is real but small, and it flips Week 11 — Tucker Kraft's bye, which lands in trade-deadline week itself — from covered to a tight end hole nothing else on the roster fills.
- **No rival lost a starter to injury overnight**, and nothing in the league's transaction log (a kicker swap here, a tight end add there) points to a new mismatch worth chasing. The one thing that would change this hunt — a rival running back going down — still hasn't happened.

## Standing watch

Trade deadline is Week 11 — four weeks out. Every remaining week still fields a legal lineup, and nothing shows a playoff-week hole that can't still be fixed on waivers before the deadline. Revisit Monday regardless, and sooner if a rival's running back situation changes.
