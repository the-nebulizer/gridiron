## The full hunt

Scanned all 12 rosters fresh off tonight's sync. Your actual surplus and need, read off the outlook rather than guessed: four quarterbacks against two starting spots, three running backs against three starting spots (zero bench cover), three receivers against two (one spare), two tight ends against one (one spare). The only position the roster can't legally cover if it loses a body is defense — and that's a Tuesday waiver-streaming question, not a trade one; it's not for sale anywhere in this league either.

**Quarterback depth looked like the obvious chip to sell.** Four teams in the league — GehrmannJake, sweeneyb, ObiWanless, amandaberry — are running exactly two quarterbacks with no bench arm at all, real exposure in a league that starts two every week. All four also happen to be sitting on running back depth you don't have. On paper, a clean match: a spare arm for a spare back.

It didn't survive the numbers. Trading away Jared Goff or Lamar Jackson for any of their bench backs (J.K. Dobbins, Bhayshul Tuten, RJ Harvey, Rico Dowdle, and three lower names) lost projected points for the rest of the season every time — Lamar still projects as a difference-making quarterback once healthy, and Goff is the arm that covers Week 13, when Cousins and Lamar are both on bye. Neither is actually spare once you look past the raw count.

**The tight end swap is the one worth writing down, because it's instructive.** Sending Juwan Johnson (bench, behind Tucker Kraft) for Bhayshul Tuten projects positive in every single window the rule checks — flat this week, +1.1 the next three, +4.4 rest of season, +2.1 in the playoffs. It would pass the mechanical bar outright. But running it through the forward outlook shows why the bar isn't just the point total: with Johnson gone, Week 11 — Kraft's bye, and the trade deadline week itself — goes from fully covered to a tight end slot nobody on the roster can fill. That's not a trade-off to accept for a modest point gain; it's trading a real, currently-covered hole for a worse one later, which is exactly the kind of move this system is built to catch before it ships. Pass.

Checked the same logic against a spare receiver (Malik Washington) too — every real running back on offer there cost points this week and over the season. Nothing clears.

## Standing watch

Trade deadline is Week 11 — five weeks out, no change in urgency yet. Nothing in the forward outlook shows a playoff-week hole that can't still be fixed on waivers before then, so there's no reason to force a deal now. Revisit next Monday with fresh usage and injury news; a bye-week crunch or an injury to a rival's starter is what turns one of these near-misses into a real offer.
