## The defense swap, checked against fresh matchup numbers

Sometime after this morning's report, Cleveland went into the starting defense slot and Chicago came out. Worth running back through the numbers, because this morning's report landed on the opposite call.

Taken as a straight season projection, Cleveland does look like the better defense this week — 8.6 points projected against Chicago's 6.6. That's almost certainly what's behind the swap, and it's a reasonable thing to go by. But a season projection doesn't know who either team is playing this week, and this league's rule is that no slot with a choice gets set on projection alone. Weighed against the actual opponents, the order flips: Chicago's home game against New England grades out at 10.3 adjusted points, ahead of Cleveland's trip to Tennessee at 9.2 — New England has given up more to opposing defenses this season than Tennessee has. That's the same read this morning's report used, and nothing has moved it: no injury news on either defense's personnel and no weather concern turned up researching both sides again tonight.

The gap is real but modest — about a point on the adjusted number, with the raw projection pointing the other way. That's a legitimate close call, not a blowout, which is exactly why it's worth saying plainly rather than either overriding the live lineup silently or leaving the swap unexamined. The recommendation is to flip back to Chicago before Thursday's kickoff; if there's a reason behind the swap that isn't in the snapshot or tonight's research, that's the one case this read can't see.
