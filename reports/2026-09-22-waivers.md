**Add Emanuel Wilson (RB, Seattle), bid $20 FAAB. Drop Mike Gesicki to open the spot.** Nothing else on the wire clears the bar this week — the kicker, defense and (now) tight end holes further out are real but not worth spending the bench's only open chair on this early; they'll get handled with closer-in streams.

## Why Wilson

Running back is the one position I'm carrying with zero cover — three active backs, three lineup spots, no body in reserve (Charbonnet's own bye-week return is still weeks off the earliest optimistic date, and he's not close to being activated). Emanuel Wilson just became the clearest fix on the wire: he's Seattle's short-yardage and goal-line back, and he took over the Seahawks backfield in Week 2 — 21 carries for 92 yards — after Jadarian Price left with a shoulder injury. Price's X-rays came back clean and the team is calling it minor, but even a full return likely means Wilson keeps a real early-down and goal-line role behind him, and if Price sits again Wilson is the clear next man up. He's not walking into my starting lineup this week — I already have three healthier backs ahead of him — but he directly answers the one spot where I have nobody behind my starters, and he's this week's most-added player in the league by a wide margin, so the price only goes up from here.

## Why Gesicki, not someone else

The bench is full and two of my other four bench spots are spoken for by trade offers already out to other managers (Cousins and Meyers to Tally241 for Aaron Jones; Bryce Young as the fallback to amandaberry for Brian Robinson) — those players can't be named as a waiver drop while those offers are still open, since either one could still be accepted before Wednesday's claims run. That leaves Keenan Allen and Mike Gesicki as the only real options. Dropping Allen would leave me exactly one receiver drop away from a real hole if the Cousins/Meyers trade also goes through this week — Meyers and Allen are both wide receivers, so losing both at once would leave me at the bare minimum with nothing in reserve. Gesicki's spot doesn't touch anything another routine has already committed to, so it's the cleaner, self-contained trade-off: it opens tight end back up instead.

## What this costs later

Running the swap forward: it doesn't touch the Week 13 quarterback picture, and Week 7's receiver situation stays intact (Diggs and Meyers are both on bye that week, but Allen is still around to cover). It does reopen Tucker Kraft's Week 11 bye — with Gesicki gone, there's no second tight end that week, so Week 11 joins Week 7 (kicker) and Week 10 (defense) as a week that needs a waiver stream before it arrives. Tight end waiver options are plentiful most weeks, so this is a "handle it closer to the bye" problem, not an emergency — but it's a new item for that same pre-bye shopping list, not a free roll.

## FAAB after this move

$92 remaining before this claim. A $20 win on Wilson leaves $72. Hold at least $20–25 of that back for the Week 7 (kicker), Week 10 (defense) and Week 11 (tight end) streams — all three are deep positions on the wire and better shopped closer to each bye than reserved for today — and keep some powder for a startable streaming quarterback if one surfaces before Week 13, since those windows close fast in this league. Spend the rest only on an obvious upgrade.
