Read off a fresh sync this afternoon. Week 2 is in the books, Week 3 hasn't kicked off, and claims process tomorrow morning — so this is the last window to change the bid that was on the card this morning.

**This report replaces this morning's.** That one led with Emanuel Wilson for $20 with Mike Gesicki as the drop. Pressure-testing it against fresh data, it fails on three counts, and one of them is a straight factual error.

## The claim made the calendar worse, not better

Run the roster forward as it stands and there are exactly two weeks you cannot field a legal lineup: **Week 7** (Cameron Dicker's bye, and he's the only kicker) and **Week 10** (Chicago's bye, and they're the only defense). Week 11 — Tucker Kraft's bye — is currently fine, because Gesicki covers it.

Run it forward *with* the claim and there are **three**: Week 7, Week 10, and now Week 11, because dropping Gesicki leaves no tight end at all that week. Tight end goes from a position with a spare body to one with neither a spare nor a coverable bye — the same shape kicker and defense are already in. The claim's only effect on the season calendar was to add a hole.

The thing that should have stopped it: **Wilson is on bye in Week 11 himself.** The week the drop breaks is a week the addition can't play either.

## The running back "hole" is a preference, not a shortage

The case for Wilson rested on running back having no cover — three backs for three lineup spots. That's true only because Jaylen Warren is sitting in the flex right now. The flex takes a receiver or a tight end just as happily, and you carry four receivers for two receiver slots. If a back goes down, Jakobi Meyers or Keenan Allen steps into the flex and the week is normal. That's why every running back bye ahead — Warren in Week 9, Bucky Irving in Week 10, Ashton Jeanty in Week 13 — still fields a full lineup without any help.

No week on the calendar is short a running back. Wilson fixed nothing and cost a tight end week.

## Charbonnet is close, and this morning's report had it backwards

This morning's write-up said Zach Charbonnet's return was "still weeks off the earliest optimistic date" and that he wasn't close to being activated. That's wrong. Seattle has him on an aggressive timetable he's currently on track to meet, the coach says he looks "dang good," and he's a candidate to come off the list as early as **Week 5** — and once he's back he's expected to be Seattle's preferred back.

Which makes the trade you were being offered this: **$20, a fifth of the budget, for two or three weeks of Seattle's second-string back — and then you drop him anyway to make bench room for the Seattle back you already own.** Wilson only had a role at all because Jadarian Price left Sunday's game; Price's X-rays were clean and his coach said Monday it "shouldn't be a long-term thing." Two of the three reasons to want Wilson could be gone by Sunday, and the third arrives in a Seattle jersey in about two weeks.

There's a quieter version of the same point: Wilson and Charbonnet share a backfield *and* a bye week. They don't stack; they cancel.

## What the bench seat is actually for

The bench is full, all five spots, and the injury slot is occupied by Charbonnet. When he's activated — plausibly Week 5 — somebody has to come off the roster to make room. Gesicki's spot is the natural seat for that, and it's the only one not tangled up in anything else. Spending it today on a two-week rental means paying for that room twice.

Hold it.

## The three weeks that do need shopping, and when

- **Week 7 — kicker.** Dicker's bye. Buy in Week 6.
- **Week 10 — defense.** Chicago's bye. Buy in Week 9.
- **Week 11 — tight end.** Only if Gesicki leaves for some other reason; as long as he's here, this week is covered.

All three are safe to defer, and this is worth saying precisely rather than as a shrug: every single team in the league carries exactly one kicker, and eleven of twelve carry exactly one defense. Nobody hoards them, so the pool doesn't thin out. Better still, the weeks you need the extra body are weeks you'll already have someone on bye to drop — Diggs and Meyers are both out in Week 7 — so the bench math solves itself if you wait.

## Budget

$92, untouched. Keep about **$25** of it earmarked: roughly $10 for the Week 6 kicker, $10 for the Week 9 defense, $5 for a tight end if one is ever needed. That leaves around $65 of real money for something that actually starts — a receiver or back who beats a current starter, or a quarterback if Lamar Jackson or Jared Goff gets hurt. A fifth of the budget on a bench back who doesn't crack the lineup is exactly the spend that leaves you short in October.

Nothing on the wire clears that bar this week.
