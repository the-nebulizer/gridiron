## The idea, and why it's not an action yet

Jackson is Out again this week — he's now missed 4 of the team's first 9 weeks — and "Out" is one of this league's IR-eligible designations (`league.ir_eligible_statuses` in the snapshot). The IR slot has sat open all season. Moving him there costs nothing in the lineup (he was projecting zero either way) and turns an occupied bench spot into a free one, which is exactly the kind of move worth making.

But it doesn't stand alone. The open Bowers trade to annaf23 (Monday's trades report) gives up Kirk Cousins — a quarterback. Running the combination through the outlook (`--add 6130 --drop 4881 --drop 1166`, using Cousins' departure as a stand-in for the trade) shows the real effect: QB would drop to 2 active bodies (Young, Goff) against a lineup that starts 2 in this superflex format. Still legal — no crunch week appears — but it goes from comfortable to zero-margin, and the two moves are controlled by different routines on different clocks (the trade has no deadline; it could sit open for days). Forcing them together into one report either overstates today's urgency or understates the trade's effect, so this week's call is to hold the IR move and revisit it the moment the trade is accepted or declined. If it's declined, Jackson goes to IR next report with no downside at all. If it's accepted, the question becomes whether a QB add is worth making alongside it — a call for whichever routine runs next after that happens.

Last Tuesday's report called Jackson's absence "a one-week loss that's already locked in." That turned out wrong — it's recurring — but the fix now runs into a different routine's open commitment, so it waits one more week rather than being forced through.

## What would have gone in that freed spot

If the bench spot opens, Devin Singletary (NYG) is the obvious candidate: he's the most-added player in the league by a wide margin after the Giants placed Tyrone Tracy Jr. on IR with a hamstring injury (minimum four games), and reporting has him taking the larger share of that backfield. He would not start here — Warren, Irving, and Jeanty hold every running-back spot this lineup fills, and running back isn't thin or uncovered in the outlook — so he'd be a stash, priced like one (a $6 bid was the plan, well under the 10% depth cap on $76 FAAB). Without a free slot, taking him this week means dropping Malik Washington, which the outlook flags as newly leaving wide receiver thin — a real downside that wasn't there before. That's not worth it for a non-starting stash, so the hold stands on him specifically, independent of the IR timing question above.
