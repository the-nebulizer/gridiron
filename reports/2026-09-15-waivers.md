## Mike Gesicki (TE, CIN) — takes the open bench slot outright, no drop, no dependency

Roster room, not FAAB, is what's actually scarce this week: one open bench spot, two candidates who want it. Gesicki wins it outright. He doesn't start over Kraft this week — but tight end is one of this roster's thinnest spots (one active body, one dedicated slot), and with him on the roster, Week 11 — Kraft's bye — has a tight end again, no drop needed. That's the strongest case on this card: fixes a real, dated hole, needs no drop, and doesn't wait on anything.

He led all Bengals pass-catchers in Week 1 (5-78-1 on 7 targets, ahead of both Chase and Higgins on the day) as Cincinnati's clear starting TE — not a committee piece, not contingent on someone else's injury. $8 buys the Week 11 insurance outright, and since tight end is thin there's no stash-price ceiling holding the bid down.

Because he takes the last open slot without touching Meyers, this claim doesn't depend on the Cousins/Meyers trade to Tally241 at all — there's no reason to tie it to how that trade resolves.

## Devaughn Vele (WR, NO) — fallback only, not the priority

Re-checked the question Ben raised directly: **who does Vele actually beat out?** Nobody. Four active WRs (McMillan, Diggs, Allen, Meyers) against two dedicated WR slots, and the FLEX is held by Jaylen Warren, a running back — WR isn't thin here. Adding him doesn't open up a start anywhere on the calendar; it would just fill the one open bench spot with nothing to show for it this week or any week ahead.

Compare the two cases directly: Vele is a fifth receiver on a team that isn't thin at wide receiver, and fixes nothing on the calendar; Gesicki fills this roster's thinnest spot and fixes a dated hole. A stash doesn't get to take the last open slot ahead of a claim that actually fixes something, however hot the wire says he is. So Vele moves behind Gesicki in the order: claim him only if the Gesicki claim is lost to a higher bid — if Sleeper awards Gesicki to another team on Wednesday and the bench spot is still open, pivot to Vele instead.

He's still worth having in that scenario: the #1 most-added player in Sleeper this week, a legitimate 7-69-1 Week 1 (9 targets, 82 snaps), and Jordyn Tyson (the Saints' presumptive true WR2) is on IR with a hamstring injury expected to keep him out into October — a 5+ week runway as a low-end flex option. Priced as the stash he is: **$10, not $15**. A player who doesn't start gets a stash price, and $10 is the ceiling for one at a position that isn't thin.

The two claims not touching the same player (Meyers stays put either way) means neither one depends on how the Cousins/Meyers trade to Tally241 resolves — that trade can be decided on its own schedule without forcing either waiver claim's hand. Keenan Allen still isn't a drop candidate for either move: with Diggs and Meyers both on bye Week 7, Allen is the only healthy WR left that week, so dropping him would open a brand-new Week 7 hole on top of the existing K/DEF one.

## Budget

$8 (Gesicki, primary) + $10 (Vele, fallback only if Gesicki is lost — never both) leaves at least $82 of the $100 FAAB untouched, and realistically $90 since only one of these two claims can land on the same bench spot. Reserved and not touched this week: roughly $10 for the Week 7 kicker/defense bye pair (both thin positions, but that bye is five weeks out — no free agent on the board today is a clear enough upgrade over Dicker or Buffalo to spend early).

## Considered and passed

- **Caleb Douglas (WR, MIA)** — trending, but a lesser role than Vele; not worth a second WR claim, let alone bumping past the stash cap.
- **Devin Singletary / Kaelon Black (RB)** — committee backfield pieces; my 3 active RBs (Jeanty, Irving, Warren) all hold real roles and RB isn't flagged thin.
- **Carson Wentz (QB, MIN)** — already four deep at quarterback (Jackson, Goff, Cousins, Young); pass regardless of signal.
- **K/DEF free agents (Loop, Santos, Gay / TB, SF, GB defenses)** — both positions are genuinely thin (1 body, 0 bench cover), but Dicker and Buffalo are both fine starters with no bye until Week 7. Nothing available today is a season-long upgrade worth spending ahead of that; the reserve above covers it when it's actually needed.
