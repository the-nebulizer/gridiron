## What I checked

Fresh sync, 10-2, no legality problems anywhere on the calendar (`node scripts/outlook.mjs` — every week through W17 fields a full lineup). The only soft spot is the kicker (`no_cover_positions: ["K"]`), and that isn't a trade question — every one of the other 11 rosters carries exactly one kicker too, so there is no partner to trade for one. That's a weekly streaming call for `/waivers`, not this report.

My one genuine trade chip is Kirk Cousins. He's quietly outscoring most starting fantasy QBs (24.0 pts/game over 6 games, 22.3 the last three) but can't crack my lineup behind Jared Goff and Bryce Young — `scripts/value.mjs` prices losing him for the rest of the season at only 2.5 points, since Goff and Young already cover every week he'd otherwise start, including his own Week 13 bye. In this league's 6-pt-pass-TD superflex scoring, a QB at that level is exactly what a thin QB room will overpay for.

I scanned all 12 rosters for the fit. Two teams are running a real QB room gap — a backup-caliber pair with no insurance behind it:

- **sweeneyb (8-4)**: Tyler Shough and Dak Prescott, no third arm.
- **ObiWanless (3-9)**: Joe Burrow and a benched Jayden Daniels, no clean second option.

Both have running-back depth I could use — I'm carrying zero bench RBs behind Warren/Irving/Jeanty. I ran every plausible version of "Cousins for a back" through `scripts/value.mjs --trade`, which checks a deal this week, over the rest of the season, and in the playoffs (W15–17):

Every single version loses points in the playoffs, and none gain anything this week. The reason is structural, not a bad pick of targets: none of the backs on offer would actually start over Warren, Irving or Jeanty — they're pure bench depth — while Cousins' own best weeks land right in the playoff stretch (he projects 18.9–25.9 in W15–17), so shipping him out gives up real emergency/bye insurance for depth that never plays. Bhayshul Tuten is the one name worth watching — a legitimate trending role in Jacksonville (61.6% snaps, 4 red-zone looks the last three weeks) — but even the best version of that deal fails on playoffs alone, and Ben's rule is no offer goes out unless every window is better, not just most of them.

I didn't find a fit the other direction either — no rostered player on another team would clearly displace one of my starters for a price my own depth (a second TE, a second streamed defense, Diggs) could actually buy.

## Looking ahead

Four weeks to the trade deadline (W11). Nothing on the calendar needs fixing before then — no bye or legality hole shows up anywhere through the playoffs. If a real buy-low shows up (an injury to a rival's starter, a role change that makes one of these backs a clear lineup upgrade rather than depth), Cousins is still sitting there as the asset to spend; nothing about holding him costs anything meaningful between now and then.
