## The two real close calls — decided on matchup and usage, not projection

**TE: Kraft over Johnson, hold.** The raw numbers favor Johnson by a hair — his projection (9.6) and matchup-adjusted estimate (7.7, against a Cleveland defense that's given up the 10th-most to tight ends) both edge out Kraft's (8.6 projected, 6.8 adjusted, against a tougher Patriots defense ranked 23rd). But that's a sub-point gap on both counts, and it's dwarfed by usage: Kraft has played 84.6% of snaps and pulled 6.7 targets a game over his last three; Johnson is at 59.6% snaps and 3.3 targets — roughly half the role. A tougher matchup on a clear lead tight end beats a nicer matchup on a part-time one. Hold Kraft.

**SUPER_FLEX: Cousins over Young, hold.** Projections are a coin flip (19.3 Young vs. 19.2 Cousins), which is why this one flags as a disagreement — but the matchup-adjusted numbers aren't close once each player's own per-game ratio is applied: Cousins is outperforming what his opponents allow by 26% this season (ratio 1.26) against Young's 10% (ratio 1.10), which pushes his adjusted estimate to 23.4 against Young's 22 even though San Francisco's raw numbers to QBs look tougher than Denver's. Cousins is also a full-time 100%-snap starter with 2.7 rush attempts a game to Young's 92.3%/1.3. Hold Cousins.

**K and DEF, both holds.** Reichard projects 9.3 against Buffalo's middling kicker defense (25th of 32) — not a great matchup on paper, but running every available free-agent kicker through the points model shows none of them score better this week once their own team's scoring environment is weighed in. Denver (8.5 projected, adjusted 10.7 against Carolina) stays over bench piece Cleveland (6.8 projected, adjusted 8.4 against New Orleans) — not close once adjusted.
