## Why Nick Folk, and why $3

The kicker pool is deep (20 unrostered options) and nobody fights hard for the position, so this is priced as a cheap fill, not a contested bid. Folk stands out on role certainty: Atlanta signed him in free agency specifically to be their 2026 starter after burning through two other kickers, he converted 28 of 29 field goals with the Jets last season, and this week he's at home in a dome against Chicago. Tyler Loop (Baltimore) had the highest Sleeper-wide add count, but Baltimore's offense has stalled with Lamar Jackson out, which cuts the other way for a kicker. Folk is the safer, equally cheap claim.

## Why Bryce Young is the drop

Every drop on the bench costs something; Young is the one that leaves every remaining week fillable:

- **Diggs stays.** He's a real weekly receiver when healthy, hurt only since Week 5, and on his bye next week anyway.
- **Lamar Jackson stays.** There's a [real chance he's back Sunday against Cleveland](https://atozsports.com/?p=500897), though Baltimore's coach calls it unknown, and he's your best quarterback when healthy.
- **Tucker Kraft stays.** Without him, Week 8 (Juwan Johnson's bye) has no tight end at all.
- **Tetairoa McMillan stays.** Without him, Week 7 (Meyers and Diggs both on bye) is a receiver short.

What dropping Young costs: **if Lamar sits Sunday**, Goff is on bye, so Cousins starts at quarterback and the extra quarterback slot goes to McMillan or Kraft — a few points, that week only. In **Week 13** (Cousins, Lamar and Jeanty all on bye) that slot is a non-quarterback too; the lineup is still legal. Quarterback depth isn't scarce on the wire right now — Daniel Jones, Indianapolis's starter, is unclaimed — so if Lamar is ruled out late in the week, he can be picked up first-come-first-serve after Wednesday's run, with a drop of his own.
