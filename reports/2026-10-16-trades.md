> Refreshed Oct 16 after a roster change: started Kirk Cousins at QB, benched Lamar Jackson

## What changed, and why it doesn't open anything

Lamar Jackson moving to the bench behind Kirk Cousins and Bryce Young is this week's lineup decision, not a change in what he's worth. He's still your best-performing healthy quarterback this season (23 points a game) and the rest-of-season model still favors him well ahead of the other two once his ankle clears — an ankle that's drawn conflicting reports (real chance to play, to out for multiple games) rather than a clear timeline either way. That uncertainty is a reason to sit him for one week, not a reason to sell low on him, and it isn't new information the trade hunt hadn't already priced in — he was already a game-time question when yesterday's hunt ran.

## Rechecked against the full league

Today's activity log shows only routine waiver churn across the league — a kicker swap here, a backup quarterback add there, nothing that signals a rival losing a starter or suddenly needing what you have surplus of. The standing read from yesterday's full 12-roster hunt still holds:

- Kicker and defense are the only real thin spots here, and nobody trades those mid-season — that's a weekly streaming question, not a trade one.
- Four quarterbacks for two superflex starting spots is real depth, but every quarterback-needy team in the league is already starting both of its own, so there's still no motivated buyer.
- No other roster is sitting on a running back or receiver that would clearly upgrade a spot in this lineup.

## Standing watch

The trade deadline is Week 11 — five weeks out, no change. Nothing on the forward outlook shows a playoff-week hole that can't still be fixed on waivers before then. The one thing worth watching, not acting on: if Lamar Jackson's ankle turns into a multi-week absence, his trade value (and the case for moving a spare quarterback instead) could look different by Monday's hunt — that's a question for fresh news, not for tonight.
