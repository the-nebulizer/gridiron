## Why Chicago is the wrong start this week

The Bears' defense is genuinely good — they held Minnesota to three field goals, 246 yards and 3-for-13 on third down. That's not the problem. The problem is the other side of their own ball: Caleb Williams is hurt, Tyson Bagent is in concussion protocol, and as of yesterday the Bears may be starting Case Keenum, who hasn't started a game since 2023. Against a 2-0 Philadelphia team, that means a defense on the field all night, chasing. Defensive fantasy scoring is mostly about game script, and this one points the wrong way.

## Why the Giants

They're home against Tennessee and Cam Ward, who has already been sacked five times in two games behind a line that isn't holding up. A pass rush with a lead is where defensive points come from — sacks, hurried throws, turnovers — and that's the setup here. It's the most-added available defense in the league this week, which is also the reason not to sleep on it.

**Carolina is the fallback, not a downgrade.** They were the highest-scoring defense in football last week — five takeaways, two sacks, a defensive touchdown, three points allowed — and they draw a Cleveland offense ranked 28th in yards that has allowed seven sacks with Deshaun Watson under center. The one caution: last week's explosion came against a third-string quarterback who then got benched for an undrafted rookie, so the unit is probably not as good as that box score. The matchup still is.

If both are gone, Detroit at home against the Jets is the next name — Geno Smith took four sacks last week — but Detroit has given up 30 and 41 points in its own two games, so it's a worse bet than either of the above.

## What the swap costs

Nothing on your bench: it's a defense for a defense, so no other player moves and no bench spot is used. The only real cost is that Chicago's own bye in Week 10 stops being your problem and the Giants' Week 8 bye becomes it — two weeks earlier, and Carolina's would be Week 5, two weeks from now. On a position you restock every week anyway, that's a note rather than a cost: there are 19 unrostered defenses today and there will be plenty then.

You do give up Chicago's friendlier looks later — the Jets in Week 4, New England in Week 7 — and if you'd rather keep them for those, the honest alternative is to sit this one out and accept a bad Monday night. I don't think that's the better side of the trade, but it's a real choice and not a close-your-eyes one.
