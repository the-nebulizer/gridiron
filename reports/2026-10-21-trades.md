## Why the hunt comes back the same

Monday's lineup move swapped starters for bye-week and matchup reasons — Jared Goff, Malik Washington, and Will Reichard in; Kirk Cousins, Stefon Diggs, and Tyler Bass out. Nobody left the roster and nobody arrived, so the counts that drive a trade hunt haven't budged: four quarterbacks against two starting spots, three running backs against three spots (no bench cover), three receivers against two (one spare), two tight ends against one (one spare), and defense as the only position the lineup can't legally cover if it loses a body — which isn't a trade problem in this league; nobody deals defenses.

Re-scanned all 12 rosters fresh off tonight's sync anyway, since a hunt means checking, not assuming:

- **Quarterback depth still doesn't survive the numbers.** The same four teams running bare at QB (GehrmannJake, sweeneyb, ObiWanless, amandaberry) are still sitting on running back surplus that would look like a clean match for a spare arm. It still isn't one: trading Jared Goff or Lamar Jackson away for any back on their benches loses points for the rest of the season every time. Jackson still projects as a difference-making quarterback once he's back, and Goff is the arm that covers Kirk Cousins's bye in Week 13. Neither is actually spare.
- **The tight end trade idea is unchanged and still a pass.** Sending Juwan Johnson for a running back still clears the points bar in every window, and still opens a brand-new Week 11 tight end hole the moment Johnson leaves — a real covered need traded for a worse one, for a modest gain. Not worth it.
- **One new data point, and it doesn't create an opening.** Jalen Hurts went to IR with a blood clot, and the manager who'd been carrying him (annaf23) cut him loose rather than stash him — which drops annaf23 to two quarterbacks with no bench arm, in theory a trade target. But their actual surplus is wide receivers, a position you're not short at, and their running back room is ordinary, not deep. No fit.

## Standing watch

Trade deadline is Week 11 — five weeks out, same as Monday. Nothing in the forward outlook shows a playoff-week hole that can't still be fixed on waivers before then, so there's still no reason to force a deal. Revisit Monday with fresh usage and injury news; an injury to a rival's starter, not this week's lineup swap, is what turns one of these near-misses into a real offer.
