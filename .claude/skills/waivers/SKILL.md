# /waivers — FAAB report (waivers process Wednesday)

1. **Sync first (mandatory).** Run `node scripts/sync.mjs`; STOP on failure. Read `data/league/snapshot.json`.
1b. **Check the add mode** (see CLAUDE.md "Calendar calibration"): if `games_have_started` is false, or recent `free_agent` transactions complete instantly, the pool is first-come-first-serve — recommend immediate $0 adds ranked by urgency, NOT FAAB bids, and say clearly that it's a race. Bid advice applies only once players are actually on waivers.
1c. **Know what's coming; still decide for now.** The snapshot's `outlook` (computed every sync — see `scripts/outlook.mjs`) carries my count at each position and two answers to "no cover": `thin_positions` (losing one body empties a dedicated slot) and the wider `no_cover_positions` (no spare body for what the lineup actually starts — the superflex QB, the flex RB — catching holes `thin_positions` alone misses), plus for every week ahead which starting slots I cannot fill. Use it as context, not as a plan: rank candidates on what they do for this week, name or dismiss the best available candidate at every `no_cover_positions` entry, and let a future hole break a tie and get named in the report. Never count positions by hand or off `dedicated_slots` alone, and never recall a bye by hand — both are already computed.
2. Candidates = the snapshot's `available` pool (the real unrostered players by position, best first), plus `trending.adds` where `rostered_in_league` is false, plus anyone in `transactions` recently dropped in THIS league. `trending` alone is Sleeper-wide noise and misses most of the pool — a listed NFL starting QB can sit unrostered without ever trending, which in superflex is the most valuable thing on the wire. If a name isn't provably unrostered in the snapshot, it doesn't go in the report.
3. Web-research the top candidates: why trending (injury ahead of them? role change?), rest-of-season outlook, not just last week's box score.
4. Recommend: up to 5 adds ranked on what they're worth now, each with a FAAB bid sized against my `faab_remaining` and the standing intents in `docs/SEASON-PLAN.md`. QBs are gold in superflex — grab any startable QB who ever hits waivers. Name the drop for each add, from my bench, with one sentence.
   One genuine forward constraint, because it's a resource and not a preference: **don't spend to nothing ahead of a week the outlook says I cannot fill.** Name the reserve as a number and what it's for. Everything else about the future is context to mention, not a rule to obey.
4a. **Say where he would actually slot, against the players I already have.** An add is worth what it changes in the lineup, nothing more. For every candidate, name the player on my roster he beats out and the slot he takes — count what I already have at that position from `outlook.roster_shape.active_by_position` against `dedicated_slots` plus the flex slots. If he would not crack the lineup this week, say that outright and call it a stash, priced like one. **"There is a free bench spot" is not a reason to spend**: the bar for a fourth or fifth body at a position I already start two of is far higher than the bar for someone who walks into the lineup. If the honest answer is "he is better than my current FLEX", say whose place he takes; if it is "he is WR5 and never starts", the bid should look like it. The answer goes in the block as `displaces` — that starter's id, or `null` — the card prints it as the Starts line, and `--check` rejects a block without it.
4c. **Roster room is the scarce resource, not FAAB.** When two candidates want the same open bench slot, the slot goes to the one whose case does something — starts this week, fixes a week the outlook can't fill, or covers a `thin_positions`/`no_cover_positions` entry. A stash never takes the last open slot ahead of a fix, however hot the wire says he is; if he's still worth having, he goes in as `if_not` the fix (claim him if that one loses) or waits for a drop. Read the case lines `--check` prints back and make sure the primary claim's lines are the strongest on the card — if a fallback's case reads better than the primary's, the order is wrong.
4b. **Say what each swap costs later.** Run `node scripts/outlook.mjs --add <add id> --drop <drop id>` for each recommended swap. If it changes anything in the weeks ahead — fixes a hole, opens one, or drops the only body at a `thin_positions` or `no_cover_positions` position — put that in the report in one sentence. The move can still be right; what's not allowed is making it silently.
5. Note explicitly: claims are manual in the Sleeper app; deadline is Tuesday night before Wednesday processing.

## Mid-week: "someone dropped X — do I care?"

Not every question is the Tuesday report. When Ben asks about a single player, or a move another manager just made, run `node scripts/league-activity.mjs` (after the sync) and answer in this order:

1. **Can he even be added right now?** Before kickoff it's first-come-first-serve, so the answer is "go take him". After kickoff a drop sits on waivers — 2-day clear, Wednesday processing, costs FAAB. Only the Sleeper app shows which state a given player is in: "Add" means instant, "Claim" means Wednesday. Say so rather than guessing.
2. **What does it cost?** With a full roster every add is a swap; the question is never "is he good" but "is he better than the 15th man".
3. **Is the position streamable?** Never carry two of K or DEF. A dropped defense is worth a claim only as a clear season-long upgrade on the starter, or to cover that starter's bye that week.
4. **Which way is the market moving?** The snapshot carries Sleeper-wide adds AND drops. A player being dropped by tens of thousands is a signal; so is the reverse. Report the net, not just the add count — they run in both directions and the add count alone flatters a player everyone is cutting.
5. **Is he actually rosterable?** A player on IR or PUP can't fill an active spot.

6. **Check standing commitments first.** Read `reports/actions.json` (per `docs/ACTIONS.md`) before naming a drop: never propose dropping a player who appears in an open `trade` action's `give` list, or who is `consumes`-listed by another open action, unless this claim is explicitly `if_not` that action. If a claim's drop only works because a pending trade offer falls through, say so in `why`, set `if_not` to that trade's id, and note that waivers process Wednesday whether or not the offer has been answered — so the claim's drop must be someone not in any open offer, or the claim is a fallback only.
7. Write the report to `reports/YYYY-MM-DD-waivers.md`, action block first per `docs/ACTIONS.md`, e.g.:
   ```actions
   { "week": 2, "verdict": "Add Justice Hill now; drop Bryce Young to make room.",
     "next_check": "Lineup, Thu 7am",
     "actions": [{ "kind": "add", "player": "<id from snapshot trending.adds>", "drop": "9228", "mode": "fcfs",
       "displaces": null, "urgency": "now", "why": "Henry handcuff with a pass-down role; Young never starts." }] }
   ```
   A hold verdict still ships this block, with `"actions": []`. Then validate the block you just wrote: `node scripts/actions.mjs --check reports/<file>.md` — strict, and STOP on failure; fix the block, never bypass. Then compile the card: `node scripts/actions.mjs`. The compile also reads the other three routines' newest reports; if it notes an action of theirs as done, gone, or drifted, that is expected — it is not yours to fix and not a reason to stop.

Never: size a bid off a player's own outlook without saying who on my roster he displaces — a free bench spot is roster room, not a reason; recommend a player without `rostered_in_league: false` in the current snapshot; spend below $1 on a player worth rostering; propose an `ir` move without checking `league.reserve_slots` and `league.ir_eligible_statuses` in the snapshot (this league has one IR slot and it only takes certain designations, so IR is usually not a way to free a bench spot); emit an action for a player the snapshot doesn't place exactly where the action claims; judge a swap on this week alone; bid more than a tenth of remaining FAAB on an add with `displaces: null` at a position that isn't thin — `--check` rejects it, and it is the exact mistake the card exists to stop.

**Publishing.** A report that isn't on `main` never reaches the dashboard. Finish with `node scripts/publish-report.mjs reports/<file>.md reports/actions.json "report: week <N> waivers" --replace` and say where it landed — see CLAUDE.md.
