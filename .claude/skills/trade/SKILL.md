# /trade — evaluate or hunt

1. **Sync first (mandatory).** Run `node scripts/sync.mjs`; STOP on failure. Read `data/league/snapshot.json` — all 12 teams' full rosters are in `teams`.
1b. **Read the league's actual rules and my forward view from the snapshot, not from memory.** `league` carries every rule a trade turns on — `trade_deadline`, `trade_review_days` (0 here: an accepted offer processes at once), `veto_votes_needed`, `draft_pick_trading` (false — picks cannot be part of an offer), `max_keepers` (this is a keeper league, so a young player has value past this season), `playoff_week_start`, and `median_matchup`. `outlook` carries my positional counts, both `thin_positions` (dedicated slots only) and the wider `no_cover_positions` (no spare body for what the lineup actually starts — the superflex QB, the flex RB — catching holes `thin_positions` alone misses), and every week ahead I cannot fill.
2. Mode A — **evaluate** (Ben pasted an offer): confirm every named player's actual roster in the snapshot first. Value both sides in THIS league's scoring (superflex + 6-pt pass TD inflates every startable QB well past generic trade charts; half-PPR). Then run the offer through `node scripts/outlook.mjs --add <each id I get> --drop <each id I give>` and compare it to the plain `node scripts/outlook.mjs`. Say in one line what the deal changes in the weeks ahead — a week it turns from fillable to unfillable is worth stating plainly. That's a cost to weigh against the value, not a veto. Never read a bye from memory. For every player I would receive, name who on my roster he displaces and the slot he takes — an incoming player who would not start is depth, and should be valued as depth, not as the headline. Put it in the block as `displaces` (the id of that starter, or `null` for depth) for the first player in `get`. Verdict: accept / counter (with the counter) / decline, in plain sentences.
3. Mode B — **hunt**: scan all 12 rosters for surplus/need mismatches against mine. My surplus and need are the outlook's `active_by_position` against `dedicated_slots` plus the flex slots — not an impression of the roster. For every `no_cover_positions` entry, name or dismiss the best available free agent first — a no-cover hole is only a trade priority once waivers can't fill it cheaper. Identify the 2–3 most plausible partners, check each candidate offer with `scripts/outlook.mjs`, and draft a short, sendable offer message for the best one. For the incoming player, name who on my roster he displaces and put it in the block as `displaces` (the id of that starter, or `null` for depth) for the first player in `get`. Trading from a position with cover into one without is worth noting when it's true; it doesn't override a better deal.
3b. **Price the surplus before selling it, and say when waiting beats dealing.** A position is only surplus against what my lineup *starts* — `outlook.roster_shape.lineup_demand`, not `dedicated_slots`. In superflex that is two QBs a week, so a third arm is the first spare and a second is depth, not a throw-in. Then count the same position across all 12 rosters in the snapshot before naming a price: when most of the league carries exactly what it starts, a spare body is scarce, and scarcity is the whole of my leverage. Scarcity also has a clock — it is worth most when a rival's starter is hurt or on bye and worth nothing after the deadline, and the bench slot it occupies is a real cost while I wait (`bench_open`). So when the best offer available today is below what the position is worth, say so and recommend holding, naming the week to revisit and what would trigger the sale. Never hold more than one spare at a position for this reason, and never hold a body the outlook shows I need in a week ahead.
4. Read the deadline and the playoff field from the snapshot (`league.trade_deadline`, `league.playoff_teams`, `outlook.weeks_until_trade_deadline`). The deadline is the one hard cutoff in the season: after it, a hole in the playoff weeks can only be fixed on waivers. So from three weeks out, say how many weeks are left, and if the outlook shows a slot I can't fill in `playoff_weeks`, name it — that is the last window to trade for it. With 7 of 12 making the playoffs the bar is low, so value wins-now; a keeper slot (`league.max_keepers`) means a young player kept past this season is real value, not a throw-in.
4b. **Read the other manager's side off the snapshot, and cite the news.** Before drafting any offer, check whether each player you want sits in their **starters**, on their **bench**, or on their **IR**, and how many bodies they carry at that position — the compiler computes this into the card's `Asks` line, so a report that contradicts it is wrong on the record. Asking a manager to pull a starter out of their lineup while sending only bench pieces is the offer that never gets answered; `--check` warns on exactly that shape, and if you send it anyway, `why` must say why they would take it. A trades report once argued an offer was easy because the player was "still on Tally241's bench" while the snapshot had him in their starting lineup — it sat unanswered for four days. Whatever injury or role claim the deal rests on goes in the block as `evidence` (`{note, url, as_of}`), or `"evidence": null` if it rests on the rosters alone; `--check` rejects a trade with no `evidence` key.

5. **Check standing commitments first** (both modes). Read `reports/actions.json` (per `docs/ACTIONS.md`) before drafting or judging an offer: prefer offers that do not include the player this week's open waiver claim intends to drop. If the best offer must include such a player, link the two — the claim becomes `if_not` the trade, or the trade runs `after` the claim — and say in `why` that the offer locks that player through Wednesday's waiver processing. Remember an offer sent Monday can still be unanswered Wednesday, and with a 0-day review an acceptance processes at once.
6. Write the report to `reports/YYYY-MM-DD-trades.md`, action block first per `docs/ACTIONS.md`, e.g.:
   ```actions
   { "week": 9, "verdict": "Send the Diggs-for-Etienne offer to roster 4.",
     "next_check": "Trade hunt, Mon 7am",
     "actions": [{ "kind": "trade", "with": 4, "give": ["2449"], "get": ["<their player id, from teams[] in the snapshot>"],
       "displaces": "<id of the starter he replaces, or null>",
       "evidence": { "note": "Etienne out-touched Bigsby 18-4 in Week 8.", "url": "https://example.com/usage", "as_of": "2026-09-22" },
       "message": "Diggs for Etienne — you need WR depth, I need a bellcow.",
       "urgency": "this_week", "why": "Fills my RB2 hole; Diggs is buried behind their top two." }] }
   ```
   A hold verdict still ships this block, with `"actions": []`. Then validate the block you just wrote: `node scripts/actions.mjs --check reports/<file>.md` — strict, and STOP on failure; fix the block, never bypass. Then compile the card: `node scripts/actions.mjs`. The compile also reads the other three routines' newest reports; if it notes an action of theirs as done, gone, or drifted, that is expected — it is not yours to fix and not a reason to stop.

Never: value a QB off a standard-scoring trade chart; propose a player the snapshot shows on a different roster than assumed; include a draft pick in an offer when `league.draft_pick_trading` is false; ignore what the OTHER manager needs (a trade they won't accept is worth nothing); judge an offer on this week's lineup alone when `scripts/outlook.mjs` will show you what it costs later; emit an action for a player the snapshot doesn't place exactly where the action claims; send two offers that give the same player without linking them `if_not`, or two that between them would leave me at or below `lineup_demand` at a position — each offer's card is computed as if the other never happened, so unlinked they both quote a roster that only exists if one of them fails. The same holds in the other direction: two unlinked offers that each bring back a body at one position, or one offer plus an open waiver claim at that position, are refused by `--check` when together they would leave me two past what the lineup starts there. Trade for a position `no_cover_positions` actually names, not one whose count merely looks tight — it re-solves the lineup without one body there, so a flex a spare receiver can fill is not a hole, and buying depth there is how a card ends up carrying three moves that chase one imaginary need.

**Publishing.** A report that isn't on `main` never reaches the dashboard. Finish with `node scripts/publish-report.mjs reports/<file>.md reports/actions.json "report: week <N> trades" --replace` and say where it landed — see CLAUDE.md.
