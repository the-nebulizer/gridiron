# /lineup — weekly start/sit

1. **Sync first (mandatory).** Run `node scripts/sync.mjs` (add a week number to target a specific week). If it fails, STOP and report the error — never proceed from an old snapshot or memory.
2. Read `data/league/snapshot.json`: my starters/bench/reserve, opponent's starters, everyone's `injury_status`.
2b. **Start from the numbers.** `node scripts/value.mjs --lineup` prints the best legal lineup by projection for this week, with injury tags applied (Out/Doubtful/IR score zero this week) and byes removed; `node scripts/value.mjs` adds each player's season average, last-3 average and games played. That is the baseline. Every slot where you start someone else needs a reason from the research below — news the projection can't know (a late scratch, a role change, weather) — stated in the report. Never set a slot on an impression of who is "better".
2c. **Read the games, not just the players.** `node scripts/matchups.mjs` groups every player I could start by NFL game: home/away, both teams' quarterbacks by depth chart with injury tags, my fantasy opponent's starters in the same game, who is on bye, and which bench players are close calls (within 3 projected points of a starter they could replace). Games marked `*` are the ones the lineup turns on. The projection already prices the opponent in as one number; this step is for what one number hides — and every player on one NFL team shares the answer, so research each game once, not each player.
3. **Web research, per marked game** (not the unmarked ones — usage discipline):
   - **Who is throwing, on both sides.** If either team's depth-chart starter is tagged (the sheet says "confirm who starts"), find out who takes the snaps. A backup moves every receiver, tight end and back on that team — and the other side's quarterback shapes the game script and decides what my defense faces.
   - **The betting line: game total and spread**, and from them each team's implied points. High total → more scoring chances for everyone in it, including my kicker. Big favourite → likely ahead and running late (good for its lead back, a little worse for its receivers); big underdog → likely behind and throwing (receivers and the QB up, the back down unless he catches passes).
   - **Pass or run lean.** How this offense splits its plays, and whether the opponent is easier to throw or run on — how the defense ranks against each position I have in that game (QB, RB, WR, TE).
   - **Kicker:** the team's implied points, whether drives tend to stall short of the end zone (field goals rather than touchdowns), and the roof and weather — wind and cold cut long kicks.
   - **Defense:** the opposing quarterback (sacks taken, interceptions, a backup starting), injuries on that offensive line, and the opposing team's implied points.
   - **Injury/inactive news** for my players and the close calls, and current-week expert consensus for players within ~2 ranking spots of each other.
   Cite where each line, ranking or quarterback status came from. Roster facts still come from the snapshot alone — the web never says who is on whose fantasy roster.
4. Decide each slot in **this league's scoring**: superflex + 6-pt pass TDs (a healthy starting QB in SUPER_FLEX nearly always beats any WR/TE), half-PPR.
5. Weigh the matchup for every starter, not only the close calls: a starter can still lose his slot to a bench player when the game around him has turned (a backup QB, a low total, a script against his role) — say so when it decides a slot, and name the game fact in the `why` ("backup QB starting; total 38"). When the matchup and the projection agree, say that in a clause and move on. Frame close calls by win probability: projected favorite → prefer floor; underdog → prefer ceiling. Say which framing you used. If the snapshot's `league.median_matchup` is true, every week is also scored against the league median, so a high floor wins twice and a bench-heavy boom week wins once — weight floor accordingly and say so.
4a. **Every slot with a choice is decided against who each player faces — a rule, not a step to skip** (Ben, 2026-10-21: first for quarterbacks, after a QB call went out on projections alone, then for every position). The matchup sheet's **Calls** table lists every position where a healthy, playing player sits on the bench — QB, RB, WR, TE, K, DEF, plus the flex starter a bench player could replace. For **every** player in it the report names: his opponent; what that opponent has allowed his position per game this season and its rank (from box scores in league scoring — for a defense, what defenses score against that offense); his opponent-adjusted estimate; his usage over the last three games (snap share, targets, carries, red-zone looks), and whether it is rising or falling; and his projection. Then it says which way each of those moved the call. When the projection and the adjusted estimate pick different players, or usage is moving against the projection, it is a close call: research those games (step 3 — the line, who's throwing on the other side, weather and roof) and decide on that, stating the framing (floor vs ceiling, and the median game). No slot with a choice may be set, or held, on projection alone — and a gap that looks close on projection but not on matchup and usage isn't close; say so. For a defense or kicker, the same applies to the unrostered options: `node scripts/stream.mjs` (and `--pos K`) prints what that position scores against each option's opponent. If the table can't be built (no snapshot `defense_vs_position`), say so and research the matchups instead — never skip it. The same goes for an `/inactives` pivot with more than one healthy option.
5a. **Set this week's lineup for this week.** Never bench a player to protect a later week — lineups are set weekly and nothing carries over. Then, separately, read the snapshot's `outlook` and close the report with one line naming the next `crunch_weeks` entry and what it needs. A slot the outlook says I cannot fill (`empty_slots`) is a waiver job; hand it to `/waivers` rather than solving it here.
5c. **Which defense and kicker start.** If two are rostered, `node scripts/stream.mjs` and `node scripts/stream.mjs --pos K` print who each one plays this week; pick on the matchup from step 3 (the team's implied points for a kicker; the opposing quarterback and implied points for a defense) and say who they play in one clause. If only one is rostered and it is on bye, the slot scores zero — that is a waiver job, so say so and hand it to `/waivers` rather than leaving the slot to look filled.

5b. Read `reports/actions.json` first (per `docs/ACTIONS.md`): a `start` action must not name as `for` a player who sits in an open trade offer's `give` list — the move is still legal in Sleeper, but say in the report that the player may leave — and lineup moves are always step 1 of the standing order, so a `start` action never carries `after`.
6. Output: the full legal 10-slot lineup (QB, RB, RB, WR, WR, TE, FLEX, SUPER_FLEX, K, DEF); for every change from current starters, one sentence of reasoning; flag any Questionable/Doubtful starter with the bench pivot to make before kickoff (lineup changes are manual in the Sleeper app).
7. Write the report to `reports/YYYY-MM-DD-lineup.md`, action block first per `docs/ACTIONS.md`, e.g.:
   ```actions
   { "week": 2, "verdict": "Start Bucky Irving over Jaylen Warren at FLEX.",
     "next_check": "Waivers, Tue 7am",
     "actions": [{ "kind": "start", "player": "11584", "for": "8228", "slot": "FLEX",
       "urgency": "before_kickoff", "why": "Warren questionable; Irving has the full workload." }] }
   ```
   The card shows a case for every move — for a start, who comes in at which slot and the benched player's live status — computed by the compiler from the block, so the `why` should carry the judgment (matchup, injury, usage), not restate the roster. A hold verdict still ships this block, with `"actions": []`. Then validate the block you just wrote: `node scripts/actions.mjs --check reports/<file>.md` — strict, and STOP on failure; fix the block, never bypass. Then compile the card: `node scripts/actions.mjs`. The compile also reads the other three routines' newest reports; if it notes an action of theirs as done, gone, or drifted, that is expected — it is not yours to fix and not a reason to stop.

Never: start a player the projection ranks lower without saying what the projection misses; name a player without confirming their roster slot in the snapshot; carry availability claims from search snippets; leave a slot empty when a legal option exists; emit an action for a player the snapshot doesn't place exactly where the action claims.

**Publishing.** A report that isn't on `main` never reaches the dashboard. Finish with `node scripts/publish-report.mjs reports/<file>.md reports/actions.json "report: week <N> lineup" --replace` and say where it landed — see CLAUDE.md.
